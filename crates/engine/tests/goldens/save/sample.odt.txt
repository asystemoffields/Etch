<text:p text:style-name="Title">The Open Studio Handbook</text:p>
<text:p text:style-name="Standard">How a small community of makers shares space, tools and time</text:p>
<text:p text:style-name="Standard">Every Thursday evening the old print shop on Alder Street fills with people who make things. <text:span text:style-name="T1">Painters</text:span> set up by the north windows, <text:span text:style-name="T2">letterpress</text:span> regulars claim the back room, and somebody always brings bread. This handbook collects what we have learned about running a shared studio: the routines that keep it pleasant, the tools we pool, and the small agreements that let thirty strangers become a crew.</text:p>
<text:h text:style-name="Heading_20_1" text:outline-level="1">Why share a studio?</text:h>
<text:p text:style-name="Standard">Rent is the obvious reason, but it is not the best one. A shared room puts your work next to work you would never have seen otherwise. A ceramicist explains glaze chemistry to a comic artist; a photographer borrows a weaver&apos;s loom for a still life. The cross-talk is the point.</text:p>
<text:p text:style-name="P1">A studio is a place where unfinished things are welcome.</text:p>
